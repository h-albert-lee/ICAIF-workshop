%% Paragraph 1: the DLP problem. Regex solves canonical PII; real text is not canonical.
Korean financial institutions are required to prevent personal (credit) information from leaving controlled systems, and increasingly deploy LLM-based DLP filters on unstructured text. For canonical strings---a resident registration number written \texttt{900101-1234567}, a card number with a valid Luhn digit---format rules and checksum checks provide a strong starting point. The difficulty is everywhere else: a customer dictating an account number as \textup{일일공 에 일이삼 에 사오육칠팔구} over the phone, a number split across two turns and read back by the agent, a scanned statement whose zeros became the letter O, a ``masked'' RRN that still exposes birth date and sex.

%% Paragraph 2: why existing benchmarks do not measure this.
Existing resources motivate a benchmark focused on Korean financial input variation. PII-Bench studies English PII~\cite{shen2025piibench}; REDACT addresses multilingual privacy~\cite{vats2026redact}. Korean resources cover synthetic conversations~\cite{fei2024kdpii,jang2024koreanpiiner} and legal text~\cite{hahm2025thunderdeid}. Our focus is the intersection of a Korean regulatory taxonomy, financial document contexts and controlled surface-form variation.

%% Paragraph 3: contributions (4 bullets max).
Figure~\ref{fig:overview} illustrates the controlled input variations and taxonomy
underlying \kiii{}. We make three contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{regulation-grounded, two-tier taxonomy} of 36 categories: 24 \emph{Legal} categories each cited to a statutory article (\S\ref{sec:taxonomy}), and 12 \emph{Identifiability} categories capturing quasi-identifiers and behavioural attributes that enable profiling.
  \item A \textbf{synthetic long-context, multi-subject corpus} of 1,440 documents and 218,664 spans in which identifiers are generated by code with applicable format and checksum checks and rendered through a controlled \textbf{surface-form variation} axis (T0--T3) informed by Korean STT conventions (\S\ref{sec:benchmark}).
  \item A \textbf{reproducible evaluation protocol} for full-context, targeted span extraction, with planned comparisons across four LLMs and three frozen baselines and breakdowns by tier, variation level, subject count and context length (\S\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}
